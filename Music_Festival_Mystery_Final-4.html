        Teacher note: how the clues lead to the answer
        1. Clue 1 puts the three instruments in the top row, so the three kinds of music are in the bottom row.
        2. Clue 2: the violin is between the drums and the oud, so it is in the middle of the top row (spot 2).
        3. Clue 3: Ibrahim’s violin and classical music are in the same column, so classical music is in spot 5.
        4. Clue 4: traditional music is directly to the right of classical music, so it is in spot 6.
        5. Spot 4 is the only bottom spot left, so jazz music goes there.
        6. Clue 5: Faris’s drums and jazz music are in the same column, so the drums are in spot 1. The oud is the last instrument: spot 3.

        Check: of the 720 possible arrangements, exactly one satisfies all five clues. The game also re-checks this in the browser console when it loads.

        Sources. The festival picture and the six card photos are all cropped from the textbook pages supplied for this activity (Unit 3: Interests, the opener page and the Vocabulary page). The festival picture is cut into six tiles, and each correct placement reveals one tile. Pieces can be dragged onto the board, or tapped and then placed with a second tap. Sentences tagged “Textbook” restate captions printed on the page (Faris can play the drums and wants to play jazz; Ibrahim wants to play the violin and classical music). Sentences tagged “Game rule” (the instruments are in the top row, between, directly under or to the right of, same column) were added by this game and are not from the textbook. The word-review sentences are the textbook’s own Vocabulary-page sentences. The 3-minute timer is a friendly challenge: at 00:00 the game carries on and nothing is lost, and the completion screen shows the real time taken and whether it was inside the limit. Hints and feedback are game text. The stage, sun and notes on the start screen are illustrations, not textbook images.
